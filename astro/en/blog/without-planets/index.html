What if the Other Planets Didn't Exist?

(Everything here is my personal opinion. If you disagree, you're probably right 'ㅅ')

There's the Sun. There's the Earth. There's the Moon.

But the universe has more neighbors.

Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

They're all orbiting the same Sun, apparently.

It doesn't really seem like they're doing anything for us. They're just spinning out there, far away.

But what if they disappeared?

Jupiter

Jupiter is the biggest one in the solar system.

This giant — it's apparently been taking hits from rocks flying in from space on our behalf.

No Jupiter? The rocks heading for Earth multiply dramatically, apparently.

Jupiter is Earth's bodyguard 'ㅅ'

Saturn

Jupiter is the biggest in the solar system, right.. but apparently having something that massive all alone is dangerous. It's so powerful it could throw the orbits of nearby planets into chaos.

Saturn apparently holds Jupiter in check from the outside.

No Saturn? Jupiter goes rogue on its own, and the path Earth travels could become a total mess, apparently.

Even the bodyguard needs a partner 'ㅅ'

Venus

Venus is apparently almost the same size as Earth.

Without Venus, the path Earth takes around the Sun shifts slightly, apparently.

The path shifts → distance from the Sun changes → temperature changes → weather changes → animals change → everything changes, apparently.

One thing changes and everything else follows.

So the life forms might not look anything like what we know now =ㅅ=...?

Mars

Even without Mars, things would be fine immediately, apparently.

But there are loads of rocks floating between Mars and Jupiter. Mars helps keep them in place. Without Mars, those rocks start flying off in all directions, apparently.

And Mars is apparently the closest candidate humanity could relocate to someday.

Mars is also a small partner to Jupiter 'ㅅ'?

Uranus, Neptune

The planets at the very edge.

Apparently they block chunks of ice at the outskirts of the solar system from drifting inward.

Without them, ice chunks from the outside come flying in freely, apparently.

Kind of like a magic shield blocking ice missiles =ㅅ=...?

Mercury

Mercury is the planet closest to the Sun.

Even without it, the direct impact on Earth is apparently almost nothing.

But here's the interesting part — Einstein apparently used Mercury to prove his theory was correct. Without Mercury, the history of physics might have gone differently, apparently 'ㅅ'

And this is just my thought.. the Sun is an incredibly hot entity. Yet Mercury is a planet with a water-element name =ㅅ=...?

Everything has a role

Jupiter = bodyguard.
Saturn = supervisor.
Venus = balance.
Mars = rock management.
Uranus, Neptune = outer guard.

Lose even one, and Earth is in trouble, apparently.

But the names are kind of strange

Ancient people named the planets.

Jupiter = Zeus. King. Protector.
Saturn = Kronos. Order. Discipline.
Mars = Ares. War.
Venus = Aphrodite. Beauty.

But the names and what they actually do are way too similar.

Jupiter is genuinely protecting. Saturn is genuinely keeping order. Mars is genuinely fighting off rocks.

How did they know 'ㅅ'?

Did they not know?

Usually people say "the ancients didn't know science."

But.

The Babylonians apparently predicted solar eclipses decades in advance.

The Maya apparently calculated the time it takes Venus to orbit the Sun with a margin of error of two hours. Without a telescope.

These are people who "didn't know" 'ㅅ'

Solar and lunar eclipses happen when the Sun, Earth, and Moon line up in a straight line..

You could also say it's a question of timing.

But to get the timing right, you need to know how the Sun moves, what speed the Moon orbits at, and where the Earth is

all of it.

And the ancients could do that =ㅅ=.....?

Thousands of years

Ancient people apparently looked up at the sky every night.

And recorded it, apparently. For thousands of years.

When Jupiter was here, the kingdom was at peace. When Saturn was here, hard times came. When Mars was here, war broke out.

I sometimes look up at the night sky.

Each of those bright dots up there. Maybe they're all there for a reason 'ㅅ'?

Anyway, it's fascinating. Really fascinating 'ㅅ'
